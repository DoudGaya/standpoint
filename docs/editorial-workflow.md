# Editorial workflow

## States

The supported progression is:

`draft → assigned → in-progress → submitted → fact-checking → copy-editing →
legal-review → approved → scheduled → published`

Published records may then move to `updated`, `corrected`, `retracted` or
`archived`. Not every story requires fact checking or legal review, but skipping
a stage must be an explicit editor decision recorded in the revision summary.

## Responsibilities

- Reporters maintain sourcing, disclosures, authorship, accuracy and revision
  notes before submission.
- Fact checkers verify material claims and record source notes.
- Copy editors review language, links, captions, alt text, taxonomy and SEO.
- Section editors own prominence, scheduling and desk-specific publication.
- Managing editors approve sensitive coverage, retractions and homepage leads.
- Legal reviewers advise on flagged content; the workflow record is not a
  substitute for privileged legal systems.

## Publication checklist

Before moving to approved or scheduled, verify:

- public headline, summary, body and canonical slug;
- primary category, topics/tags and correct edition/language;
- author/contributor roles;
- hero alt text, caption and credit;
- source/disclosure/sponsorship fields;
- content warnings where needed;
- links and embeds use HTTPS and approved providers;
- SEO title/description and social image;
- correction or original-publication context where applicable.

Scheduling uses UTC datetimes. Editors should confirm the newsroom display
timezone before a high-impact release.

## Corrections and updates

Routine changes after publication use `updated` and a clear revision summary.
Material factual corrections use `corrected`, a public correction block or
note, timestamp and responsible editor. A retraction requires a public reason
and should preserve the original URL unless counsel and editorial leadership
approve a different response. Separate `correction` documents support a public
corrections log and audit context.

## Breaking and live coverage

Breaking ticker items require start/end times, priority, enabled state and
either an internal story or a reviewed HTTPS destination. Live entries are
chronological, author-attributed and can be pinned or marked key/critical.
Corrections attach to individual live entries instead of rewriting history
silently.

## Commercial separation

Sponsored stories require advertiser attribution and a visible disclosure.
Campaign and placement records never grant commercial users access to ordinary
editorial content. Native advertising should use distinct presentation,
labelling and approval rules documented by the publication before launch.
